KRIKET

A NEXT-GENERATION CRICKET SIMULATION — THE MANUAL

A full cricket match — bat, bowl, field, run — built on real physics and running entirely in your browser on Three.js (WebGPU, with a WebGL2 fallback). Every delivery is a live physical event: seam presentation, conventional and reverse swing, spin, pitch interaction, the bat–ball collision, fielding, throwing and running between the wickets. Nothing is scripted; the scoreboard emerges from the simulation.

04 / CAMERAS

A broadcast-style director cuts between frames. Batting has three personalities — pick yours with V.

📺 Broadcast (TV)

the classic

High behind the bowler, tight 33° lens, drifting subtly with the play. The frame you grew up on — and the frame the bowling UI is built around.

🏏 Behind the batter

default when you bat

Over the off-side shoulder with the stumps in shot — the picture is you defending your wicket. Keeper crouched behind, bowler attacking down the length of the pitch. Pans with the ball, never loses the pins.

🪖 Helmet cam

first person

Rides the striker's actual head — sway, crouch and all. Watches the bowler's hand until the ball is live, then tracks it in. As close to facing 140 as a browser gets.

🎯 Ball-follow

on contact

The instant bat meets ball the director cuts to a panning follow that widens as the shot travels — like a real TV operator riding the boundary.

💥 Wicket zoom

on a dismissal

Hard cut to the stumps as the bails fly (they're simulated too, disturbed by the ball's actual velocity).

🔁 Side-on replay

wickets & boundaries

The delivery re-rolled from the side-on analyst position — watch the swing you just misread in full.

Camera rule of honour: no camera ever chases a parked ball. Frames only track the ball while it's genuinely live — in flight or in play.

05 / THE SIMULATION

A fixed 120 Hz integrator steps the ball through air, pitch and contact. Regulation everything: 156 g ball, 22 yards, 71 cm stumps, a 96.5 cm bat with the sweet spot 45 cm from the handle.

AERODYNAMICS — WHAT THE AIR DOES

Quadratic drag

Air resistance grows with speed² — the 140 km/h thunderbolt arrives slower than it left, and the lobbed catch keeps hanging.

Magnus lift

ω × v on a spinning ball: top-spin dips, back-spin floats, side-spin drifts. Why the leg-spinner's fizzed leg-break drops on you.

Conventional swing

The angled seam trips the airflow; effectiveness peaks near a 45° presentation. Strongest with the new ball, fading as the shine goes.

Reverse swing

A rough, worn ball at genuine pace swings the other way — late, and into the toes. The death-over weapon the scoreboard never sees coming.

Weather

Overcast and humid air amplifies all of it (×1.25 / ×1.5). Captains: bowl first under clouds.

Ball wear

Overs and runs scuff the ball (you can see it darken and rough up). Wear feeds swing reversal, grip and bounce.

THE PITCH — WHAT THE DECK DOES

At the bounce, the surface takes its cut: vertical energy kept (restitution), Coulomb friction gripping the ball, seam catching and deviating, spin biting, and the odd shooter or riser from variable bounce — livelier on worn, dry surfaces. Footmarks and cracks are baked into the strip because they're part of the story.

CONTACT — BAT MEETS BALL

Timing commits at your press; the bat then intercepts the ball along your line read, and a real collision resolves the result: the middle screams, the edge flies (fine or thick, genuine catches for the cordon), the mistimed chip floats where the fielder just was. A bat–pad gate decides whether a beaten ball threads through to the stumps or finds the pad — which is how both bowleds and honest LBW shouts emerge.

FIELDING & RUNNING

Fielding

Reaction times, trajectory prediction, cut-off angles, dive stops, fumbles, difficulty-scaled catches and drops, flat throws — and overthrows when the shy misses everything.

Running

The sim weighs the fielder's return time against your crossing time. A dive buys you 1.8 m. Direct hits are adjudicated on the stumps' actual geometry.

The honest-randomness contract

Everything stochastic — seam deviation, bounce variance, execution error — rolls from a seeded RNG (mulberry32) at match start. Same seed, same match. QA replays whole games headlessly; there are no dice under the table.

08 / THE HUMANS

Every player on the field is a Xirtus HumanKit human — no capsules, no billboards.

Parametric & seeded

Each cricketer is generated from their player id on the canonical XIRTUS_HUMANOID_V1 skeleton: height, build, skin tone, face with real eyes, hair. Same id, same person, every match.

Kitted out

Team kits printed with actual surnames and numbers; pads, gloves, helmets and caps as socket-mounted equipment. Whites and long sleeves for Tests, colours under lights.

74-clip animation vault

Locomotion, sport and cricket-specific clips: the side-on stance with two-handed grip, six shot types, the bowling action, keeping crouch, appeals, celebrations, all five umpire signals.

Heads in the game

Ball-tracking IK keeps heads and eyes on the play — fielders watch the ball, the batter watches your hand. Batters render at HERO tier; the helmet cam borrows the striker's skull.

09 / THE AI

Three brains share the field against you.

🎯 The Bowler Brain

Phase-aware plans: attacks with the new ball while it swings, hits the corridor on a seamer, darts in yorkers at the death. Execution error scales with the bowler's rating — and your difficulty.

🧠 The Batter Brain

Sector discipline (won't chase the fifth-stump line forever), gap-finding against the set field, and resource management: it knows the required rate, balls left and wickets in hand.

🧢 The Captain

Reactive fields. Score freely in one sector and a sweeper appears there; new batters get catchers; set batters get the ring pushed back.

In watch mode the three brains play each other at pure club level — a genuinely self-playing broadcast with live, 2× and 4× playback.

11 / THE STRATEGY GUIDE

Hard-won wisdom from the QA nets.

BATTING

Watch the hand, not the pitch

Release tells you length. The helmet cam exists for exactly this — commit early, once.

Respect the corridor

The fourth-stump line is a question with one right answer: leave or defend (Z). Aiming a drive at it is how edges carry.

Use the whole wheel

Aim with the field: the captain posts sweepers where you're scoring. Milk the gap he's left you.

Charge with intent

Hold ↑ only against the spinners you read — get beaten and the keeper does the rest. Stumped is the loneliest walk.

Sweep the turner

On a Dry Turner, meet spin on the full or sweep it. Hanging back is how the shooter bowls you.

Run on the arm

The run sim is honest: a diving stop still delays the return. Take the second to the deep fielder's wrong side.

BOWLING

Hit release at the peak

The second SPACE sets execution error. Early or late and the plan leaks line and length.

New ball, full and straight

Swing is strongest early — especially overcast or humid. Make them play at everything for two overs.

Old ball, pace on, straight

Wear brings reverse at genuine speed: aim at the toes, not the corridor.

The slower ball is a length

Same action, later arrival. Bowl it full-ish at a set batter who swings early — the mistimed chip finds the fielder.

Spin: flight is the bluff

Leg-spin drifts and dips; the googly looks identical and turns in. Alternate them at the same line and let Magnus do the talking.

Free hits: yorker or bust

After your no-ball in a white-ball game, the stumps are the only safe scoring zone — for you.
